The mathematics behind the animation

One physical model, three perspectives

A single stationary base station transmits a narrowband carrier to a moving train. Ray 1 reaches the receiver directly. Ray 2 propagates via a fixed trackside reflecting/scattering point. The receiver travels along +x, and its four antenna elements lie along the transverse −y direction, spaced by λ/2. The source and reflector locations use meters relative to the train at time zero.

What changed: All ray directions, Doppler shifts, propagation phases and array steering phases now follow from the same transmitter/reflector/train geometry. The additional reflection phase φR is adjustable. Because the paths have different lengths, their initial phases are generally not aligned; deep fades need not fall at exactly 0.5 ms.

For small train movements, the Doppler difference approximates the beat frequency. The instantaneous fading can depart slightly from a perfect sinusoid as the arrival angles vary. Changing the reflector's position changes both its final arrival angle and the total propagation delay.

For the 1×4 SIMO receiver, perfect-CSI maximum-ratio combining captures gain ‖h(t)‖². Stale MRC achieves |h(0)ᴴh(t)|² / ‖h(0)‖². Predictive MRC uses the correct nominal geometry but intentionally adds opposite Doppler biases of ±error Hz to the two paths.

Try this sequence: Start with ≈1 ms beating. Drag the reflector to change the beat period. Change its power and phase to explore deep versus shallow fades. Select Matched Doppler to make the local Doppler frequencies equal at time zero. Scrub to examine phasors, four-branch power and stale/predictive MRC. Finally set the prediction error to zero: predictive MRC should match perfect MRC.

Important limits: The reflecting point is a simplified stationary scatterer, not a validated specular wall with electromagnetic boundary conditions. Relative ray powers are user-selected rather than predicted from reflection coefficients and path loss. Paths are modeled as narrowband phasors, so geometric excess delay is displayed but not simulated as intersymbol interference. Link B retains a constant-power single-ray benchmark. All four antenna samples and nominal geometry are assumed available, with no RF synchronization, blockage, mutual coupling or estimation overhead. These plots depict effective channel gains—not measured BER, packet reliability or an operational 6G design.
