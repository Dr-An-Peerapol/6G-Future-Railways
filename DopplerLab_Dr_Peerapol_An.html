The mathematics behind the animation

One physical model, three perspectives

We model two narrowband rays received by a train moving along the x-direction. The angle α is measured from the train's direction of travel, with its sign indicating the side of the track. The four antenna elements are spaced by λ/2 along the transverse y-direction.

Per-ray DopplerfD,ℓ = (v / λ) cos(αℓ)
Four-antenna steeringan(α) = exp(−jπ n sin α), n = 0…3
Channel vectorh(t) = ∑ℓ=1,2 bℓ a(αℓ) exp(j2πfD,ℓt)

At the first antenna, equal-power rays produce 1 + cos(2πΔf t) power fluctuations, where Δf is their Doppler-frequency difference. The periodicity is 1/|Δf|. With unequal ray powers, the fluctuations remain periodic but their minima need not reach zero.

A four-antenna maximum-ratio combiner with perfect instantaneous CSI achieves gain ‖h(t)‖². A stale combiner based on h(0) instead achieves |h(0)ᴴh(t)|² / ‖h(0)‖². The predictive curve uses the correct path geometry with deliberately mismatched Doppler estimates.

Try these experiments: Start with ≈1 ms beating and scrub to 0.5 ms. Observe the single-antenna fade while four-antenna MRC remains useful. Then choose Same Doppler: the two rays have equal cos(α), so there is no temporal beating even though their spatial signatures differ. Finally, increase the Doppler estimation error and watch predictive MRC degrade.

Model boundaries: This is a teaching model, not a railway propagation standard. We assume fixed path directions/amplitudes, no blockage, a narrowband signal, white receiver noise, and access to all four complex antenna samples. The perfect-CSI result is an optimistic benchmark. Doppler is defined for a stationary-scatterer geometric model; the original earlier ±30°/1 kHz exercise independently specified path Dopplers and array angles, whereas this demo derives both consistently from the same ray directions. Magnitudes here are channel-power gains, not measured BER or verified packet reliability.
